## R1. Sampling rate

Five signals per limb (thigh, shank and foot tilt, foot acceleration, foot height), each averaged over the last m samples and kept every m-th sample (m = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6), so the rate drops from 60 Hz to 30, 20, 15 and 10 Hz with the samples aligned on the trailing lift-off. Per signal: maximum, minimum, mean, SD, last value and time of the maximum over the window. The foot height was integrated at 60 Hz; at a lower IMU rate the integration itself would be less accurate, which is not simulated here.

## R4. Prediction sets with guaranteed coverage (conformal)

Instead of one class, the device returns the set of heights whose probability is high enough: class c is in the set when 1 - P(c) <= q, with q the ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))-th smallest nonconformity score 1 - P(true class) of OTHER participants' crossings. To keep the calibration honest, those scores come from models trained without the tested participant either (nested leave-one-participant-out). Target coverage 1 - alpha = 80 / 90 / 95 %; classes in the order H1 < H2 < H3.

| 95% | 94.6% | H1 99%, H2 92%, H3 93% | 1.14 | 85.0% (80.4%) | 14.6% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.4% |

**Reading.** At a 90% target the sets cover the true height in 91.2% of crossings with 0.98 classes on average: 97.7% of the crossings get a single height (right in 91.2% of all crossings), 0.0% a pair of neighbouring heights, 0.0% a pair of non-neighbouring heights and 2.3% an empty set (no height plausible enough: no cue). At 95% the pairs rise to 14.6%. A cue can therefore name one height most of the time and a pair of neighbouring heights when the model is unsure, with a coverage that holds for a new person.
